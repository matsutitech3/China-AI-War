\chapter{The Strategy in Whole: The Elements on the Playbook}
\label{ch:assembled}

Part~I distilled a playbook from three campaigns and then, in a chapter of equal weight, marked the terrain on which the playbook has failed. Part~II takes the elements of AI one at a time---the model and the harness that has become its contested layer, the compute, the semiconductor, the watt, the data, and physical production---and each chapter closes with its own assessment. Before it does, this chapter states the strategy in whole, because the elements are easier to read once the reader knows where each sits: it places every element on the six steps and nine moves of Chapter~\ref{ch:playbook}, marks which steps are complete, which are trending, and which have not begun, and states why the consequences of Part~III are the ones they are. It is a map drawn before the territory. Nothing in it is new evidence; every claim below is argued and graded in the chapter it points to, and where a figure is drawn from Part~III it is cited. A reader who prefers evidence before conclusion may go directly to Chapter~\ref{ch:model} and return here afterwards; the map reads the same in either order. It is placed first because the precedent chapters record what happened when each of the earlier campaigns was read one layer at a time and each layer found unthreatening, and this book does not want to invite that reading.

\section{The strategy in one paragraph}

The Chinese AI strategy is the playbook of Chapter~\ref{ch:playbook} run on a fourth industry, with one adaptation that the earlier campaigns did not need and that makes this one faster: the rent layer being attacked is digital, so the commoditizing move---openness---costs nothing to reproduce and propagates at the speed of a download. Target selection chose the discontinuity where the incumbent's accumulated advantage is about to be stranded (HBM-centric superclusters, closed frontier weights, a licensed instruction set). Demand before supply was arranged by mandate rather than by feed-in tariff (state-funded data centres restricted to domestic silicon; western hub electricity discounted only for approved domestic chips)~\cite{cac-ban,datacenter-mandate}. Overcapacity as strategy is being funded across accelerators, DRAM, and compute halls at multiples of current demand, with the losses landing where they landed in solar. Selection, not planning, is visibly at work in a model layer where ten laboratories release at frontier scale, and in a chip layer whose dozen-vendor fragmentation the state is now removing by mandate and open specification rather than by cull (Chapter~\ref{ch:compute}). Commoditizing the adversary's rent layer is the move the whole of Part~II documents: open weights, an open harness, an open ISA, open interconnect and framework specifications, at marginal or zero price, aimed precisely at CUDA, NVLink, x86/Arm licensing, and the metered API~\cite{action-plan,ai-plus,riscv-policy,huawei-ub}. And the endgame---consolidation and gatekeeping---has begun on the consolidation side (the anti-involution campaign~\cite{anti-involution}) and not yet on the gatekeeping side, where the earlier campaigns supply the template (battery-technology and rare-earth export controls of 2025~\cite{mofcom-battery-controls,csis-rare-earth}). The West's two counter-patterns, tariff walls and chokepoint denial, are behaving as Chapter~\ref{ch:playbook} said they would: the first is irrelevant to a digital good, and the second is decaying by substitution and by architectural detour.

Three of the rows deserve a sentence each on how they interlock, because the interlock is the strategy. The model and the compute are the coupling that Chapter~\ref{ch:thesis} called P1$\times$P2---adoption meeting sovereign, capacity-first hardware: open weights are a commons only for those with the memory to hold them, and a capacity-first hardware class---the commodity-DRAM socket of Section~\ref{sec:lpddr6}, the appliance of Section~\ref{sec:appliance-cost}---is what turns a download into a deployment. The watt is the weld: a tariff instrument that pays only for domestic silicon converts an energy surplus into hardware volume and hardware volume into tokens priced below the Western marginal cost, which is the demand-before-supply step of the playbook executed with electricity rather than a feed-in tariff. And the semiconductor is the floor beneath both, incomplete at exactly one layer---HBM---which is why the memory arbitrage exists at all: the architecture was chosen so that the one input the chokepoint strategy still holds is the one the architecture needs least.

\section{Scoring the campaign against the six steps}

\textbf{Step 1, target selection: complete.} The discontinuity was correctly identified and is being exploited on three fronts at once. The book's own theory chapter (Section~\ref{sec:io}) says why the choice was correct: rent is being relocated, not abolished. China opens the layers where it is behind or where openness buys adoption---weights, ISA, interconnect, framework---and expects to recapture on hardware volume, domestic cloud, standards, and dependence; the United States keeps the layers where it is ahead.

\textbf{Step 2, demand before supply: complete at home, contested abroad.} At home the state has arranged demand by mandate and by tariff. Abroad the demand is real but not yet paying: Chinese models carry roughly 61\% of one neutral router's tokens and a fraction of its revenue~\cite{openrouter-2026,volume-revenue}, enterprise open-weight spend share fell during the year usage rose, and the Global South adoption channel exists as usage before it exists as procurement. This is the one step whose foreign half is where the trust and certification barriers of Chapter~\ref{ch:analogy} bite, and Part~III's opening chapters are about that half.

\textbf{Step 3, overcapacity as strategy: under way in DRAM and compute halls, and already producing the step's characteristic pathology---with two differences from the precedents.} State-subsidized compute is running at 20--30\% utilization against a 60\% target, by some reports up to 80\% of new capacity idle, and rental prices have collapsed~\cite{china-idle,china-idle-dc}. That is what the step looked like from inside during solar's 2011--2013 and EVs' 2018--2025, and the losses land where they landed then. But two things differ. In the accelerator layer the constraint is still supply, not demand---Ascend output is bounded by HBM (Chapter~\ref{ch:semi})---so overcapacity there has not yet been possible; and Chapter~\ref{ch:strategies} records that the model-layer price war is step~3 running without a visible step-5 rent-recapture plan attached, which is the strategy's soft underbelly rather than its purchase of a learning curve.

\textbf{Step 4, selection: working in the model layer, beginning in silicon.} The model layer is the purest instance of Darwinian domestic competition the playbook has ever produced---the state did not anoint DeepSeek; it flooded the sector, and a dozen laboratories now release at frontier scale on a cadence of weeks. The chip-vendor shakeout is starting. The one policy that could damage this step is the state's own anti-involution consolidation, and it is registered as a falsification branch in Chapter~\ref{ch:synthesis}.

\textbf{Step 5, commoditize the rent layer: the step this campaign has run furthest, and the one whose completion is contested.} Every proprietary Western layer has an open Chinese counterpart shipped at marginal price. What has not yet happened is the second half of the step---the destruction of the rent that finances the Western frontier. The first direct observation of transmission arrived on 31~July 2026 with an 80\% closed-frontier price cut in the week Kimi K3's weights and DeepSeek V4-Flash shipped~\cite{openai-cut}; the company attributes the cut to efficiency, and Section~\ref{sec:rsi-branch} shows that the same datum supports the recursive-improvement counter-argument, so both readings must be printed. One cut is not a trend, closed labs also sell service rents that commoditize slowly, and elasticity may rescue the buildout even under falling prices. Proposition~P3 is graded ``mechanism operating, magnitude contested,'' and Chapter~\ref{ch:trade} prices what follows if it completes.

\textbf{Step 6, endgame: begun on one side.} Consolidation has started in the model-price war and in compute; gatekeeping has not been exercised on any AI layer. Chapter~\ref{ch:nonaligned} treats the continuity risk this implies for every country adopting the commons as a risk rather than an accusation, and Chapter~\ref{ch:consortium} prices the insurance.

\section{Where the analogy tells us to look}

Chapter~\ref{ch:analogy} scored AI against the variables that separate the playbook's victories from its defeats and concluded that the model and compute elements sit mostly in the kill zone---modular, fast-clocked, codified, self-certifiable, benchmark-verifiable---while three properties resist: the moving frontier, ecosystem lock, and trust and certification. The chapters that follow confirm the placement. The model layer behaves exactly as a modular, fast-clocked artifact behaves under the playbook: release lag compressed from months to weeks, adoption followed price, standards migrated to Chinese repositories. The compute layer behaves as an intermediate case: chiplet products and open specifications iterate quickly, while the tool chain beneath them behaves like lithography always has and is routed around rather than conquered. And the three resisting properties are precisely the ones the elements cannot settle from inside---the top US--China gap is 2.7\% while the closed-over-open gap has reopened to 3.3\%, and either could compound; the enterprise deployment layer is still Office, not modules; and the commons ships at trust Level~0--1 into a world whose paying workloads will require Level~3--4.

That is why, once the elements have been examined, Part~III opens where it opens. The deployment layer is where the precedents predict a long grind rather than a collapse, and the deployment layer is made of trust, security, and rules. Chapter~\ref{ch:trust} treats the exposed flank of openness---supply-chain integrity, behavioural integrity, provenance---and the certification ladder that would close it. Chapter~\ref{ch:order66} treats the security bill the commons has not paid, which is structural rather than national and falls due at exactly the correlated scale that success implies. Chapter~\ref{ch:governance} treats the rulebook, which the earlier campaigns' winners inherited and which is being written now in arenas most Western attention is not directed at. Only after the deployment layer is understood does the book turn to the rent side of the ledger---Chapter~\ref{ch:trade}, which prices what happens to a capital structure long scarcity when step~5 completes---and then to the electorate that will decide the outcome: the non-aligned majority of the world (Chapter~\ref{ch:nonaligned}), the strategies available to every actor class including the presumptive winner (Chapter~\ref{ch:strategies}), the scientific prize that dominates the value case (Chapter~\ref{ch:prize}), the compute a nation's science actually needs (Chapter~\ref{ch:sizing}), and the institution that all of those threads terminate in (Chapter~\ref{ch:consortium}).

\section{What the assembly does and does not license}

Assembled, the elements license one claim and forbid another. They license the claim that the AI campaign is a coherent execution of the same strategy that won three technology wars, that it is further along than a layer-by-layer reading suggests, and that its remaining gaps---HBM, packaging, tools, software maturity, trust---are being engineered around rather than closed on the West's terms. They forbid the claim that the outcome is settled. On the evidence the following chapters lay out, one step of six is complete, one is complete at home and contested abroad, three are trending, and the endgame has barely begun; the single integration test that would move P2 from ``trending'' to ``done''---a frontier-class model trained end to end on domestic silicon, packaging, and memory---has not been passed; and the three properties on which the playbook has failed before are the three on which Part~III has to do its work. The chapters of Part~II now supply the evidence, element by element; Part~III says what follows if the assembly is right, and what would show that it is not.
