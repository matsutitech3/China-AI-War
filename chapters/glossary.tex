\markboth{NOTATION AND GLOSSARY}{NOTATION AND GLOSSARY}

This book draws on economics, computer architecture, semiconductor manufacturing, electrical-grid engineering, security research, and science policy, each of which brings its own vocabulary and its own habits of abbreviation. Several of them use the same letters for different things. What follows is the reference apparatus: the notation conventions, then the labelling schemes, then the mathematical symbols by chapter, then the acronyms, then the terms of art. Nothing here is required to follow the argument---every quantity is also defined where it is first used---but a reader who arrives at Chapter~\ref{ch:sizing} wondering what a BGPU is should not have to hunt for it.

\section*{Notation conventions}

\textbf{GPT.} Economists have used \textbf{GPT} for \emph{general-purpose technology} since the early 1990s. Throughout this book that sense is always written out in full; the bare acronym GPT always denotes the OpenAI model family.

\textbf{The suffix discipline.} Six disciplines cannot share twenty-six letters without collision, and relying on context to separate them is not good enough for a document meant to be checked rather than read once. The rule adopted here is therefore mechanical: \emph{no bare single letter carries two different meanings across the book}. Where two fields would have claimed the same letter, one of three moves is made, and the reader can rely on all three.

\emph{First, a roman suffix names the referent.} A subscript set in upright roman type is a label, not an index: $f_{\mathrm{heavy}}$ is the heavy-user fraction, $f_{\mathrm{front}}$ the frontier-workload share, $e_{\mathrm{INT8}}$ the INT8 efficiency, $d_{\mathrm{duty}}$ the activity-duty product, $r_{\mathrm{disc}}$ the discount rate, $T_{\mathrm{exit}}$ the exit horizon. Italic subscripts, by contrast, are running indices ($i$, $j$, $k$, $g$, $t$, $v$) and mean what indices normally mean.

\emph{Second, a superscript names the physical dimension where two quantities share both letter and subscripts.} Electrical power is written $P^{\mathrm{el}}_{\bullet}$ throughout Chapter~\ref{ch:compute} and Appendix~\ref{app:lpddr}---$P^{\mathrm{el}}_{\text{facility}}$, $P^{\mathrm{el}}_{\mathrm{memory}}$---so that bare $P$ can remain the quality-adjusted price of Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} and Chapter~\ref{ch:trade} without ambiguity. Gross funnel output is $U^{\mathrm{gross}}$, distinguishing it from the net uplift $U$ that the same chapter reports.

\emph{Third, a distinct letter or an alternative alphabet is chosen outright} where suffixing would be clumsy. Trust in the differentiated-competition model is $\tau$, not $T$, leaving $T$ for time and token streams. Fixed development cost is $K_i$, not $F_i$, leaving $F$ for arithmetic rates and facility equivalents. Decode throughput is $\Theta_m$ and $\Theta_s$, not $R_m$ and $R_s$, leaving $R$ for revenue and rent. Fleet availability is $\alpha$, not $a$, leaving $a$ for addressability in the AI4S funnel. Service-tier population share is $\varphi_i$, not $s_i$, leaving $s$ for speed-up. The frontier-expenditure share is $\Sigma_{\mathrm{front}}$, leaving the science-production metric $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}$ as the only $S$-family object. In the security fault tree the common-cause \emph{events} are set in calligraphic type---$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sys}}$, $\mathcal{M}$, $\mathcal{B}$, with beta factors $\beta_{\mathcal{M}}$ and $\beta_{\mathcal{B}}$---so that an event is never mistaken for a scalar.

Two conventions are deliberately \emph{not} disambiguated because they are unambiguous by type. Probability is always written with the operator $\Pr(\cdot)$ rather than a letter. And the letter $C$ is cost everywhere in the mathematics; the claim identifiers CR-01\dots CR-12 of Appendix~\ref{app:dashboard} are an alphanumeric label, never a variable.

\section*{Labelling schemes}

The book runs several labelled systems. They are independent, and this is the index:

\begin{description}\itemsep2pt
  \item[Evidence grades {[V] [P] [A] [M] [R] [S]}] --- how well a claim is supported: \textbf{V} verified against primary or benchmark evidence; \textbf{P} primary claim by a party with direct knowledge (vendor, filing, government); \textbf{A} analyst estimate; \textbf{M} author-modeled; \textbf{R} roadmap or schedule; \textbf{S} scenario judgment. Defined in Section~\ref{sec:evidence}; the complete ledger is Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}.
  \item[Propositions P1--P4] --- the four load-bearing claims, stated and falsified separately in Section~\ref{sec:propositions}: adoption (P1), sovereign compute (P2), rent destruction (P3), financial repricing (P4).
  \item[Security evidence Levels A--D] --- Chapter~\ref{ch:order66}: \textbf{A} controlled technical demonstration; \textbf{B} observation on a real system or released artifact; \textbf{C} confirmed in-the-wild incident; \textbf{D} hypothesis or policy inference. Distinct from the evidence grades above.
  \item[Certification Levels 0--5] --- Chapter~\ref{ch:trust}: the graded assurance ladder from hash-identified artifact (0) to critical-infrastructure assured (5).
  \item[Parameter classes {[U] [M] [T] [P]} and {[F]}] --- Chapter~\ref{ch:sizing} only, and \emph{not} the evidence grades: \textbf{U} universal, \textbf{M} must-measure, \textbf{T} transferred, \textbf{P} policy choice, \textbf{F} future-scenario modifier. These classify model \emph{inputs} by portability across borders.
  \item[Standards-maturity stages 1--4] --- Chapter~\ref{ch:governance}: de jure specification, open reference implementation, conformance suite, installed-base default.
  \item[Playbook moves M1--M9 and steps 1--6] --- Chapter~\ref{ch:playbook}: M1--M9 are the tactical inventory of Table~\ref{tab:playbook}; steps 1--6 are the strategic sequence they serve.
  \item[Claim IDs CR-01--CR-12 and falsification branches (a)--(f)] --- Appendix~\ref{app:dashboard} and Section~\ref{sec:falsify} respectively.
\end{description}

\section*{Terms of art}

\begin{description}\itemsep3pt
  \item[Prefill and decode] The two phases of transformer inference. \emph{Prefill} processes the whole input prompt at once and is compute-bound; \emph{decode} generates output one token at a time, re-reading model weights and attention state for each, and is memory-bandwidth-bound. Almost every architectural argument in this book turns on the difference.
  \item[Roofline; operational (arithmetic) intensity; ridge point] A performance model in which achievable speed is bounded by $\min(\text{peak compute},\ \text{intensity}\times\text{bandwidth})$. \emph{Operational intensity} is arithmetic performed per byte of memory traffic; the \emph{ridge point} is the intensity at which the binding constraint switches from bandwidth to compute. Workloads below the ridge are memory-bound.
  \item[The knee] The bandwidth below which a design's achievable utilization falls sharply---in this book, the $\sim$5\,TB/s requirement of the capacity-first memory architecture.
  \item[All-reduce, all-to-all] Collective communication patterns. \emph{All-reduce} combines values across all devices and returns the result to each; \emph{all-to-all} exchanges distinct data between every pair, and is what mixture-of-experts routing requires.
  \item[Expert parallelism, expert imbalance] Distributing a mixture-of-experts model's experts across devices. \emph{Imbalance} is uneven token routing, which idles devices and is the sparse workload's most under-reported failure mode.
  \item[Wright's law / learning curve] The empirical regularity that unit cost falls by a constant percentage for each doubling of cumulative production. The engine of every Part~I precedent.
  \item[Jevons paradox] The observation, from Jevons on coal, that efficiency gains can \emph{raise} total consumption of a resource when demand is sufficiently elastic. In this book's terms, the case $\epsilon>1$.
  \item[Involution (\emph{neijuan})] The Chinese term for self-defeating internal competition: firms in a saturated sector expending ever more effort for shrinking margins. The ``anti-involution'' campaign is the state's attempt to consolidate such sectors.
  \item[Tape-out; known-good die; chipkill] Releasing a completed chip design to manufacturing; a die tested as functional before packaging; a memory-protection scheme surviving the failure of an entire device.
  \item[Pareto frontier] The set of options none of which can be improved on one dimension without worsening another---here, the cost-versus-capability frontier of available models.
  \item[Busbar; firm power; curtailment; interconnection queue] A \emph{busbar} is the physical junction where generation meets the transmission network, and the point at which delivered price and available capacity are actually determined. \emph{Firm} power is capacity guaranteed available when called; \emph{curtailment} is generation deliberately not taken, usually because transmission or demand is unavailable; the \emph{interconnection queue} is the backlog of projects awaiting permission to connect. A \emph{capacity auction} procures firm capacity for a future delivery year.
  \item[Vintage] A cohort of data-centre capacity built in the same period, sharing hardware generation, contract terms, and financing cost---the unit at which returns should be assessed.
  \item[Neocloud] A specialist provider renting accelerator capacity, typically financed by debt secured on the hardware itself.
  \item[Abliteration] A community technique that identifies and removes a model's refusal or suppression behaviour by editing activations, without full retraining.
  \item[Sim-to-real] The transfer of robot policies trained in simulation to physical hardware, and the gap that transfer exposes.
  \item[Estimand] The quantity a study actually estimates, as distinct from the quantity of interest---the distinction that makes national compute estimates comparable or not.
  \item[Notified body] Under EU law, an organization designated to assess conformity before a product may be placed on the market. The institutional form this book argues is missing for model artifacts.
  \item[Chokepoint decay] This book's term for the erosion of a control's effectiveness as stockpiles, diversion, substitution, and architectural redesign accumulate.
  \item[Mate~60 moment] Shorthand for a public demonstration that a control was ineffective, after the 2023 Huawei handset built on domestic 7\,nm-class silicon. Used here for the first frontier model trained end-to-end on a domestic stack.
\end{description}
