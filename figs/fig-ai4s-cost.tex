\caption[The AI4S expenditure asymmetry]{Why the token price is the science budget. With a fraction $f$ of
physical work served by purchased frontier tokens at relative unit price $\rho$, the share of total expenditure that
work consumes is $\Sigma_{\mathrm{front}}=f_{\mathrm{front}}\rho/((1-f_{\mathrm{front}})+f_{\mathrm{front}}\rho)$. The
workload share is a \emph{modelled} portfolio share anchored in a discussion record [A/M]~\cite{consortium-record} and
corroborated by the sizing framework's measured token layer at 14.3--14.6\% of Regime~S national demand
(Chapter~\ref{ch:sizing}); it is not a metered measurement. At $f_{\mathrm{front}}=0.146$ and the two assumed premium
cases, a one-seventh workload share becomes 77.4\% ($\rho=20$) or 91.1\% ($\rho=60$) of the budget. The lower curve is
the 3.25$\times$ serving-efficiency scenario, $f_{\mathrm{front}}=0.045$ [F/S], at 48.5\% and 73.8\%. Selective
routing of only a tenth of the token layer to the premium tier moves the same four cases to 33.1\%, 54.1\%, 12.0\% and
24.5\% (Table~\ref{tab:ai4s-cases}). The curve's shape, not its exact position, is the policy argument: at $\rho$ near
unity---which cheap open weights on owned hardware deliver---the problem dissolves, and the institution that solved it
that way has acquired a dependency it never negotiated.}
